Summary

In this episode, Arnold Bennett opens with a familiar scene: the friend who earns a good income yet never seems to have much to show for it. From there he turns to a different kind of budget. Newspapers argue endlessly about how to live on a set amount of money, but no one writes about how to live on twenty-four hours a day. Bennett calls the daily supply of time a miracle: it is equal for everyone, cannot be stolen, and cannot be borrowed against the future. He argues that money is common and fixable, while a mismanaged day muddles a whole life. He closes by asking who truly lives, rather than merely exists, on their daily time, and by noting that we will never have more time than we have now.
